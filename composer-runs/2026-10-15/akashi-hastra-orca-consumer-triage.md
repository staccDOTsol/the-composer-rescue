## Atomic envelope and payout reachability

The observed `RefreshLeg` **instruction itself** has 58 account metas and 58 distinct accounts including its program. A compact current v1 replay of that instruction succeeded at slot 457356555: **2,072 bytes, 59 keys including payer, one signer, 177,877 CU**. The specific Orca SwapV2 instruction used in the already-tested PYUSD/USDC route adds four unique keys, bringing the producer+consumer union to 62. Adding only the six unique zero-fee KLend USDC reserve/market/supply/fee/program/Instructions-sysvar keys brings it to **at least 68**, before flash authority and payout. The campaign's current v1 harness enforces 64 account keys and 4,096 bytes, so the full **KLend flash → Orca move → Akashi refresh → payout → reverse → repay** path cannot fit this exact instruction/account configuration. This is an account-count result for the sampled Hastra leg, not a claim about every Akashi leg or flash provider.

A separately constructed, outsider-funded **Orca swap → Akashi RefreshLeg/AaiValue → reverse Orca swap** executed on the current binaries at slot 457356556 as a **2,292-byte v1 transaction with 62 keys, 3 instructions, one required signer, and 264,158 CU**. That one signer is the caller who owns the USDC/PYUSD accounts and pays the fee; the Akashi refresh did not require a privileged executor signature. It moved the caller's 100 USDC forward and 90 PYUSD back; the caller went from 3,642.811711 USDC + 0.004721 PYUSD to 3,632.808482 USDC + 9.988308 PYUSD, about **0.019642 near-dollar units lower**. This shows a same-transaction pool move can reach valuation, but does not show a payout or profit. [The exact v1 simulation](akashi-orca-v1-no-flash.json) records both controls and account bounds.

The observed Akashi `Deposit` instruction is nine metas. Its unmodified union with the preceding Orca+Hastra refresh is 68 keys; even after substituting the same outsider as depositor and reusing their USDC account, the vault authority, share mint, user share account, and vault idle account keep the route at **at least 66**. The observed `RequestWithdrawal` and `FundWithdrawal` unions are 67 and 69 keys before substitutions. Thus the specific known value-moving Akashi instructions cannot be appended to this full Hastra-leg v1 route under the 64-key limit. A different leg, minimized account layout, or future deployment could change that bound.

A bounded read-only scan of **all 915 returned successful Akashi transactions** spanning slots 455628006–457346447 found 972 `RefreshLeg`/`AaiValue` calls, 248 `SyncVault`, 34 `Deposit`, one `RequestWithdrawal`, one `FundWithdrawal`, and **zero `ClaimWithdrawal`**. Current landing history therefore supplies deposit and internal funding controls, but no final user-payout control. The `FundWithdrawal` at slot 456848778 moved 1,008,773 raw USDC from vault idle to a vault payout escrow; it did not pay an arbitrary outsider. The `Deposit` controls debit a depositor's USDC and mint that depositor shares. No complete outsider-accessible refresh-to-withdrawal payout is established. A valuation report alone does not transfer or establish extractable value.
