See the [finding](residual-high-liquidity-finding.md) and [live-ELF receipt](high-liquidity-live-elf.json).

- **Units and atomic limits:** the existing-pool claim gap is one six-decimal USDC base unit, or **0.000001 USDC**. For the normal non-overflow rounding path, each effective checkpoint erases less than one raw token unit; more fee growth must occur between effective updates. A v1 transaction has a 64-entry executed-instruction trace including CPIs and a 1.4 million CU cap. See [the units and transaction-limit note](CLAIM-SCALE-AND-TX-LIMITS.md). The older 26,230 SOL route used fee-charging Save and therefore does not satisfy the zero-fee-only constraint.
- **Full one-sided boundary with an atomic return:** a separate signed v1 transaction on a mainnet-backed local fork borrowed 300 WSOL from KLend and 50 WSOL from Project 0 at zero lender fees, crossed all observed C7 lower liquidity ranges with nine Whirlpool swaps, returned through Jupiter Router V2 on an independent Raydium venue, and repaid both loans. It committed at 515,653 CU; the actor lost **257.483336497 WSOL**. See [atomic route report](JUPITER-EXIT-ROUTE.md) and [receipt](jupiter-atomic-full-boundary.json). No mainnet transaction was sent.

The zero-fee, full-range 3PRT pool moved to 10× its starting spot for 0.211995 USDC input on another fork, but retained active liquidity and only dust executable inventory. - **Two owned LPs, giant flash, both withdrawals:** a signed local-fork v1 funded actor-owned full-range positions with about $800 each in 3PRT and BSdd, borrowed **6,271.347260340 SOL** from zero-fee KLend and Project 0, swapped across both pools, withdrew both owned positions, and repaid both lenders. The actor ended down **0.405425139 SOL and 182.235402 USDC** versus its LP deposits, roughly **$224.60** at an illustrative 104.50 USDC/SOL. The remaining pools gained exactly those token amounts in aggregate. See [dual-owned report](DUAL-OWNED-FLASH-CONTROL.md), [ledger audit](dual-owned-flash-ledger.json), and [small-pool economics](SMALL-POOL-SCREEN.md). No mainnet transaction was sent.

## Residual high-liquidity grief after the merge

The merged `Partial` checkpoint calculation still loses claims when a single update credits at least one whole fee unit **and** leaves a fractional remainder. It advances the checkpoint by `ceil(owed_delta × 2^64 / position_liquidity)`. That ceiling can consume more growth than the credited whole units represent. This is reachable through permissionless `update_fees_and_rewards`; no position NFT signature is needed.

The [high-liquidity real-swap test](surfpool_high_liquidity_grief.test.ts) ran against both exact source-built binaries. A pool held `2^64` liquidity units; the target position held 75% and a second position held 25%. Each of 20 real 334-unit A-to-B swaps generated two raw A fee units and advanced fee growth by two Q64 units. Both LPs then collected from the actual SPL token vault.

| Binary | Target update timing | Target collected | Other LP collected | Total collection/vault debit | LP fee units generated |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Before | Outsider after each swap | 20 | 10 | 30 | 40 |
| Before | Once at end | 30 | 10 | 40 | 40 |
| After | Outsider after each swap | 20 | 10 | 30 | 40 |
| After | Once at end | 30 | 10 | 40 | 40 |

The exact [before](high-liquidity-real-swaps-pre.json) and [after](high-liquidity-real-swaps-post.json) receipts show ten **smallest-unit** amounts of synthetic token A left in the vault without a position credit in the repeated-update cases; the outsider does **not** receive them. Here `floor((3/4) × 2) = 1`, while `ceil(2^64 / (3·2^64/4)) = 2`, so each update credits one unit and consumes both growth units. An end-only update credits 30. The fix handles subunit-only updates, but this whole-plus-fraction case survives. The same full swap/update/collection fixture [passed against the exact current mainnet ELF](high-liquidity-live-elf.json) after initializing its test pool and hot-swapping the independently verified deployed bytes. The [test](surfpool_high_liquidity_live_elf.test.ts) checked real `Swap` and `UpdateFeesAndRewards` logs and vault debits, so this result is not based solely on source-built binaries.

The fixture uses locally minted tokens and a deliberately large liquidity level. An independent [discriminator-verified finalized mainnet scan](mainnet-liquidity-scan.json) found 6,668 active Whirlpool pools with liquidity at least `2^64` out of 164,243 pool accounts; the selected live WSOL/USDC pool has liquidity about `7.15×10^14`, far below `2^64`. A [targeted position scan](mainnet-high-liquidity-positions.json) found one active LP position in each of six sampled pools near or above that level; their shares were 100%, not the fixture's 75%. The scan identifies pools with relevant arithmetic ranges, but it does not establish profitable or material production impact. One near-`2^64` USDC pool had only 0.158970 USDC in its USDC vault when checked.

## Real deposit and withdrawal boundaries

The [liquidity-boundary receipt](liquidity-boundary.md) compares owner-signed `increase_liquidity` and `decrease_liquidity` around 13 real swaps, with and without outsider fee updates. Before the fix, the target collected zero with repeated outsider updates and two units without them. After the fix, it collected two in both cases. A liquidity change deliberately settles fees at the **old** liquidity and moves the checkpoint fully to current growth; this prevents earlier growth being credited at a newly deposited liquidity amount. An outsider cannot trigger a victim's standard deposit or withdrawal without the position NFT authority.

The [flash receipt](FLASH-REPRO.md) also contains a committed, signed transaction-v1 on a local mainnet-backed fork with three genuine flash loans, real Whirlpool swaps, a deposit of 1,000,000,000 liquidity units, a fee update, a withdrawal of the same liquidity, and all three repayments. The token CPI transfers and final account balances prove the deposit and withdrawal executed. The position NFT remained with the actor, and all lender principal vaults returned to their starting balances.

In the [existing-pool differential](REAL-POOL-DIFFERENTIAL.md), an attacker-owned position was opened *after* the victim's fee-claim gap, then increased by 100,000,000 liquidity units, updated, collected, and fully withdrawn on both branches. Its fee collection was zero. Its final token A and USDC balances, as well as both pool vault balances, matched across branches despite the victim's one-raw-USDC claim difference. Adding liquidity to the vault did not assign its uncredited balance to the new position.

## Flash transaction limits

The most intense **committed** local v1 transaction completed six full swap → update → reverse swap → update cycles inside one Save/KLend/Project 0 flash-loan transaction: 32 instructions, 2,347 wire bytes, 734,208 CU. Seven cycles failed Solana's instruction-trace limit at 751,286 CU. This is a local fork transaction, not a mainnet submission. See [the signed-wire receipt and run instructions](FLASH-REPRO.md) and [the raw committed transaction record](real-flash-cycle-optimized.json).

The separate [economic size sweep](economic-max-sweep.js) uses signed v1 **simulations** to route much larger WSOL amounts through two real swaps and both position updates while repaying the three lenders. Its Save-heavy branch copied four verified Save reserve/config accounts from the other local mainnet-backed fork into the sweep fork so the lender state is consistent. Results therefore describe that local fork state, not an unmodified mainnet snapshot. The largest sampled three-lender pass was **21,640 SOL** input (21,638.9 Save + 1 KLend + 0.1 Project 0 SOL borrowed), with all principals repaid, 1,395,344 of 1,400,000 CU and a 1,896-byte signed v1 wire. A 21,645 SOL neighbor failed at the CU cap; this is a sampled bound for this route, not a global maximum. The [adjacent tick-array receipt](economic-max-combined-adjacent.json) has each transaction result and token-account delta. The [large-flash digest](ECONOMIC-MAX.md) distinguishes committed and simulated cases.

The [two-lender variant](economic-max-two-envelope.js) omits Project 0 and prepares the actor's token accounts in a separate local setup transaction. It retains genuine Save and KLend flash borrow/repay, two Whirlpool swaps, and a permissionless position update inside the single flash transaction. A **23,080 SOL** case [committed to the first local fork](economic-max-two-adjacent-prepared-23080-send.json): `getTransaction` version 1, error null, 1,398,893 of 1,400,000 CU, 9 instructions, and 1,368 signed bytes. Nine consecutive integer-SOL trials from 23,081 through 23,089 SOL failed at the CU cap **on that fork snapshot**.

A second, fresh local fork with a different live pool snapshot [committed **26,230 SOL**](economic-max-two-adjacent-prepared-send.json): `getTransaction` version 1, error null, 1,397,368 CU, 9 instructions, and the same 1,368-byte wire size. Save lent 26,229 SOL and KLend lent 1 SOL; both principal vaults returned to their starting balances, while Save collected a 10.4916 SOL fee. The actor's pre-swap WSOL balance was 524.60001 SOL, below its first 26,230 SOL swap input, so the flash principals were actually required by this transaction. Sampled 26,240–26,900 SOL cases reached the CU cap. These bounds apply to the tested pool snapshots and route; they are not a global maximum.

This 26,230 SOL route used a fee-charging lender and only one position update on the deep WSOL/USDC pool, whose liquidity is far below `2^64`. It demonstrates atomic v1 flash capacity, **not** the residual high-liquidity checkpoint grief or an attacker harvest. The user's later zero-fee-lenders-only constraint requires a separate run; the fee-charging result does not satisfy it.

An initial Surfpool replay of the downloaded mainnet ELF executed a 138-CU placeholder (`Hello from my program!`) despite matching ProgramData bytes. Its 0/0 output was invalid. Refreshing the unchanged executable Program account with `surfnet_setAccount` forced Surfpool to recompile the loaded ELF. The real instruction then logged `UpdateFeesAndRewards`, consumed 7,848 CU, and produced owed `4` after 20 rounds with the same fractional-growth input. A full fresh-pool test against that production binary stopped at `InitializeConfig` because the local test funder lacked production authorization; see `live-full-auth-fail.log`. The source-built before/after binaries completed the full pool, swap, and collection state transitions above.

## Reproduction notes

1. Build each exact source revision with `cargo build-sbf --offline` and start a separate Surfpool 1.6.0 instance with `surfpool start --offline --no-deploy --port <RPC_PORT> --ws-port <WS_PORT> --no-tui`.
2. Load each `.so` with `python3 deploy_and_verify.py http://127.0.0.1:<RPC_PORT> /absolute/path/to/whirlpool.so`. The script refreshes Surfpool's executable cache after writing the ELF. Inspect a test transaction's logs to confirm actual Whirlpool execution; the ProgramData byte check alone is insufficient.
3. In the after checkout, run `anchor idl build -p whirlpool -o target/idl/whirlpool.json -t target/types/whirlpool.ts`, `yarn install --immutable`, and build the `@orca-so/common-sdk` and `@orca-so/whirlpools-sdk` workspaces. Copy the provided test file into the path named above.
4. From `legacy-sdk/whirlpool`, run `SURFPOOL_RPC_URL=http://127.0.0.1:<POST_PORT> EXPECT_FEE_OWED_A=4 node ../../node_modules/vitest/vitest.mjs run tests/integration/v1-ix/surfpool_pr1396_repro.test.ts --config vitest.config.mts`; repeat against the before port with expected value `0`.
