# PR #1396 residual high-liquidity fee griefing

## Finding and scope

PR #1396 preserves a position's fractional fee/reward growth when a permissionless `UpdateFeesAndRewards` call computes **zero** whole units. When a call computes **one or more** whole units, the new `Partial` path still rounds the consumed Q64 growth *up*. For a large position, that can consume growth worth a fractional whole token on each outsider-triggered update. Repeated calls can therefore reduce what its owner can later collect. This is an **incomplete mitigation of an inherited accounting behavior**: the pre-PR program also exhibits this result, because it always advanced the checkpoint to current growth. It is not a demonstrated PR-introduced regression or a third-party token harvest.

The same helper is applied to fee A, fee B, and each reward. A liquidity-changing deposit/withdrawal uses `Full` mode and advances the checkpoint to current growth; that owner-authorized transition can also discard a fractional remainder, but is distinct from the permissionless `Partial` path. The update instruction has no position-authority signer in its account list; collecting fees does require the position authority and NFT token account. [Post-PR helper](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/position_manager.rs#L15-L62), [rounding branch](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/position_manager.rs#L78-L105), [permissionless update accounts](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/instructions/update_fees_and_rewards.rs#L9-L21), [collect authority check](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/instructions/collect_fees.rs#L10-L42), [pre-PR checkpoint behavior](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/fb19ae2c894d7d4d8025d6e530c52ef6cc579859/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/position_manager.rs#L16-L46).

## Exact arithmetic

Let `Q=2^64`, pool active liquidity `Lpool=Q`, target position liquidity `Ltarget=3Q/4`, and a second position have `Lother=Q/4`. A real swap charging two raw A units of LP fees increases global A growth by `floor(2Q/Lpool)=2` Q64 units. The target's interim update computes `q=floor(Ltarget*2/Q)=1` raw A unit. The post-PR checkpoint increment is `ceil(q*Q/Ltarget)=ceil(4/3)=2`, consuming the full growth step. If the update follows each of 20 swaps, the target receives 20 units. If it is updated only once after all 20 swaps, it receives `floor(Ltarget*40/Q)=30`. The other LP receives 10 when updated at the end in either case. The pool's swap fee-growth calculation uses integer division exactly as in this example. [Swap fee-growth source](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/swap_manager.rs#L271-L291).

For fixed position liquidity `L` and ordinary non-wrapping growth, **one positive partial checkpoint strands less than one raw unit** of that token's ideal fractional entitlement. Write `r=L/Q`, integer growth `d`, credited units `q=floor(rd)`, and consumed growth `a=ceil(q/r)`. If `r<1`, then `0≤r·a−q<r<1`. If `r≥1`, integer `d` implies `d−1<q/r≤d`, so `a=d` and `0≤r·a−q=rd−floor(rd)<1`. The small loss can accumulate across distinct fee-producing intervals: in the observed near-`Q` USDC case, 20 interleaved updates produced a 19-raw-unit claim gap versus one end-only update. Increasing flash principal does not multiply the per-checkpoint rounding residue; additional effective updates need intervening fee growth and transaction capacity.

## Executed offline SBF reproduction

The [test source](surfpool_high_liquidity_grief.test.ts) creates a tick-spacing-1 pool with two active `[-1,+1]` positions, executes 20 actual `Swap` instructions of 334 raw A input, optionally interleaves a separately signed outsider `UpdateFeesAndRewards` after each swap, then updates and executes real SPL fee collection for **both** LPs. It checks that every swap increments fee growth by exactly two Q64 units. The first post-PR [Swap receipt](high-liquidity-real-swaps-post.json) logs the Whirlpool `Swap` instruction at 31,148 CU; the first outsider update logs `UpdateFeesAndRewards` at 7,463 CU. The fixtures locally minted 5e15 raw units of each test token; the `Q` liquidity position pair requires about 9.22e14 raw units of each token and fits the Solana `u64` token amount limit. This is isolated Surfpool execution, not a mainnet transaction or proof that capital is available in a particular live pool.

A narrower [existing-pool local-fork differential](REAL-POOL-DIFFERENTIAL.md) then executed against **that same deployed pool and sole active position**, rather than a new modeled pool. Two independent mainnet-backed Surfpool forks started from identical pool and position snapshots, including a baseline update. Each sent two signed, local B-to-A swaps of 20,000 raw USDC input, which advanced global B growth from 94,674 to 94,678 in two +2 steps. With an outsider update after each swap, the existing position's owed B increased from 94,637 to **94,639** (+2); with one update after both swaps, it increased to **94,640** (+3). Both branches had identical final USDC vault and swapper balances (198,970 and 10,000 raw units respectively), so the one raw USDC difference is uncredited claim, not outsider payout. The [interleaved](real-mainnet-high-liquidity-interleave.json) and [end-only](real-mainnet-high-liquidity-endOnly.json) receipts include successful Whirlpool swap/update logs. These were local fork transactions with a locally funded swapper; **no mainnet transaction was sent**, and the measured difference is 0.000001 USDC.

To test whether a deposit could capture that uncredited unit, an attacker-owned NFT position was opened *after* the gap on both forks, increased by 100,000,000 liquidity units, updated, collected, and fully withdrawn. All signed local transactions succeeded. The actor collected zero fees; its final token A and USDC balances, and both pool vault balances, were identical across branches while the victim's one-unit claim difference remained. [The negative-control receipts and source-linked outflow audit](REAL-POOL-DIFFERENTIAL.md) show that adding liquidity did not turn this vault residue into an attacker entitlement. A separate [three-lender signed-v1 flash transaction](FLASH-HARVEST-CHECK.md) executed real swaps plus an attacker-owned deposit and withdrawal; that position also ended with zero fees owed, and an explicit `CollectFees` simulation transferred zero.

For the economically sized WSOL/USDC route, a separate finalized [pair scan](mainnet-wsol-usdc-pools.json) at slots **458871678–458871680** found 31 pools, 24 with positive active liquidity. `Czfq3x...` dominated the other pools by spot virtual WSOL liquidity (about 2.33 million SOL versus about 5,334 SOL for the next pool), with about 194,796 SOL and 8.23 million USDC in its vaults at that instant. These spot figures do not simulate slippage or tick crossings for a large round trip; they only show that no other currently active WSOL/USDC Whirlpool pool was comparably deep at the sampled tick.

## Practical limit and next validation

No mainnet transaction was sent. The tested flash loans and attacker-owned deposit/withdrawal did not turn the residual into attacker profit; the separate [harvest-path analysis](harvest-path-analysis.md) found no mechanism in this rounding path that credits the lost claim to the caller or another LP. The concrete effect established here is a permissionless reduction of a position owner's later collectible fee amount in a high-liquidity numerical regime, including one raw USDC unit on an existing pool's locally forked state. A material real-world claim would require a victim and pool with meaningful fee flow, a large executable swap sequence within the pool's tick ranges, and measured economic loss relative to transaction and swap costs.
