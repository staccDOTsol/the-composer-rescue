# Fee-claim units, economic scale, and Solana v1 transaction limits

## What the fee numbers mean

Whirlpool stores `fee_owed_a` and `fee_owed_b` as integer `u64` token **base units**. Q64.64 fee growth is fractional internally, but a collectible claim is a whole base unit of that token. The token mint sets decimal display precision. The observed existing-pool fork has USDC as token B with six decimals: its one-unit owed-claim difference is **0.000001 USDC**. The synthetic two-LP fixture's 10-unit difference concerns newly minted local test tokens, with no asserted dollar value. [Whirlpool position layout](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/state/position.rs#L20-L34), [Solana mint decimals and base units](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/basics/create-mint), [existing-pool fork](REAL-POOL-DIFFERENTIAL.md).

In that existing pool, `fee_rate=100` means **one basis point (0.01%)** because Whirlpool stores hundredths of a basis point. Each tested B-to-A swap input was 20,000 raw USDC units (0.02 USDC) and generated two raw USDC units of swap fee (0.000002 USDC). Two swaps therefore generated four micro-USDC of fees while an interim outsider update erased one micro-USDC of the LP's *later claim*. The swapper exchanged USDC for the other pool token; the fork did not measure a profitable round trip. Repeating an unchanged two-fee-unit step many times at liquidity just below `2^64` would asymptotically require about two raw USDC fee units for each one raw unit of claim lost, before transaction fees, price impact, or funding costs. The live pool will move, so this is a conditional scale estimate, not a profit forecast. [Fee-rate units](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/state/whirlpool.rs#L19-L30), [fork receipts](real-mainnet-high-liquidity-interleave.json).

Let `Q=2^64`, position liquidity `L>0`, growth since checkpoint `d`, credited whole units `q=floor(Ld/Q)`, and post-PR `Partial` checkpoint advance `a=ceil(qQ/L)` when `q>0`. On the successful non-overflow path, the fractional entitlement erased by that one checkpoint is `δ=La/Q−q`, and **`0≤δ<1` raw token unit for every L**. If `L<Q`, `δ<L/Q<1` follows from the ceiling. If `L≥Q`, `q=floor((L/Q)d)` implies `a=d`, so `δ` is the fractional part of `(L/Q)d`. This follows directly from [the helper](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/position_manager.rs#L78-L105).

That `<1` bound is on **fractional entitlement consumed per checkpoint**, not on an entire sequence's final integer difference. For `K` successful updates ending at the same growth, the sum credited is at least the sum of floors of the `K` fresh growth intervals, which is at least one final floor minus `K−1`; hence the claim gap is at most **`K−1` raw units** with fixed position liquidity and no overflow. The 20-step local test at just below `Q` lost 19 raw units. A single update only after all fee-producing swaps has no differential versus one end-only update at that same time. The code's multiplication-overflow branch can discard more than this; that is a separate inherited path and is excluded from this normal-path bound. [Existing-pool local fork](REAL-POOL-DIFFERENTIAL.md), [post-PR helper](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/position_manager.rs#L84-L105).

For **six-decimal USDC**, losing a whole 1 USDC of claim by this normal mechanism would require more than one million effective checkpoints of the same fixed-liquidity position, each separated by fresh fee growth. If each outsider update were a separate one-signature transaction, the base fee alone would exceed **5 SOL** for that many calls (5,000 lamports each), before swaps, priority fees, or other costs. Packing calls into fewer transactions reduces base-fee count but needs fee-generating swaps between effective updates and consumes the 64-entry execution trace. This is a conditional cost illustration for USDC, not a bound for tokens with other decimal counts or values, and no tested path pays the caller the lost claim. [Solana fee structure](https://solana.com/docs/core/fees/fee-structure).

If the attacker generates the fees with their own swaps, lender fee zero does not remove the swap fee, price impact, or Solana transaction fee. The erased claim remains in the vault and is not credited to the attacker. An attacker-owned LP position can collect only its own fee share; this does not reassign the victim's remainder. [Separate harvest-path analysis](harvest-path-analysis.md).

## How many instructions fit in one v1 transaction?

Solana's current published v1 format permits a **4,096-byte** signed wire, **up to 64 top-level instructions**, and 64 inline account addresses (96 only if SIMD-0596 activates). The runtime separately limits the **entire executed instruction trace to 64 entries, counting top-level instructions and all CPIs**. The transaction compute cap is **1,400,000 CU**. A v1 message must explicitly set its CU and loaded-account-data limits in message config; legacy/v0 ComputeBudget instructions do not configure v1. These are simultaneous constraints, so there is no universally usable count of Whirlpool swaps or fee updates. [Solana v1 format](https://solana.com/docs/core/transactions/versioned-transactions), [trace limit and atomicity](https://solana.com/docs/core/transactions/transaction-pipeline), [compute budget](https://solana.com/docs/core/fees/compute-budget).

The local signed v1 receipts give practical examples, not protocol maxima:

| Tested local route | Top-level instructions / wire | Compute | Outcome |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Prepared three-lender (including fee-charging Save), six 1-SOL round-trip cycles | 32 / 2,347 bytes | 734,208 CU committed | Passed; flash principals repaid. |
| Same three-lender route, seven cycles | 36 / 2,493 bytes | 751,286 CU before error | `MaxInstructionTraceLengthExceeded`; 64 total trace entries binds before CU or wire. |
| Two-lender 26,230-SOL route | 9 / 1,368 bytes | 1,397,368 CU committed | Compute nearly exhausted by two large swaps; one update after them. |

Both example flash envelopes include **Save**, whose observed fee vault gained **10.4916 SOL** in the 26,230-SOL route. Neither meets a zero-fee-lender-only requirement. They are evidence for v1 wire, trace, and CU behavior, not qualifying zero-fee flash economic routes. The large route's two swaps and one update were all in one local transaction, with that update occurring after both swaps; the receipt does not isolate a PR residual loss or attacker harvest. [Six/seven-cycle receipts](real-flash-cycle-optimized.json), [large-route receipt](economic-max-two-adjacent-prepared-send.json), [route note](ECONOMIC-MAX.md).

The large-route logs show each Whirlpool swap invoked the SPL Token program twice, consuming trace entries in addition to the top-level swap. Flash-loan programs also invoke other programs, so a message with fewer than 64 top-level instructions can hit the 64-entry trace ceiling. Even with a zero-fee lender, a chain of fee-producing swaps and outsider updates is bounded by those CPIs and by CU; it cannot scale the micro-unit claim loss into a material amount in one transaction merely by increasing flash principal.
