## Two owned positions, flash funding, and the return leg

The dollar budget needs two interpretations. **$1,600 total across two pools** with balanced starting amounts means roughly `$800 TVL` in each pool, or `$400 SOL + $400 USDC` per pool. **$1,600 in one pool** means `$800 SOL + $800 USDC` in that one pool. For a pair of full-range positions, the formulas below use `$1,600 total TVL` and hence `$800 combined quote reserves`; existing third-party pool reserves add to this figure but remain owned by their LPs. A zero-fee flash can supply trade inventory temporarily; it cannot create permanent capital or offset a losing swap.

Let pool 1 receive B (USDC) and pay A (SOL), and pool 2 receive that A and pay B. Assume both start at the same fair spot, have zero fee, and have quote reserves `r₁` and `r₂`. A USDC flash input `B` yields

`B_out = B / [1 + B(1/r₁ + 1/r₂)]`.

Thus `B_out < B` for every positive trade and approaches `1/(1/r₁+1/r₂)` as `B→∞`. With `$800` total quote inventory across the two actor positions, equal split `r₁=r₂=$400` maximizes output, and that route limit is just **$200 USDC**. If there were `$1,600 TVL in each pool`, the corresponding ideal route limit would be `$400`. Unequal split makes `1/r₁+1/r₂` larger and route output smaller. This is ordinary price impact even with **zero** LP fee, protocol fee, and lender fee. Pool account reserves and fees can make exact CLMM rounding differ by raw units.

For a tangible `$800 TVL` in each wholly actor-owned pool, start with `$400` worth of SOL and `$400` USDC in each. Flash-borrow `800 USDC`, sell it into pool 1, then sell the received SOL into pool 2:

| Step at the initial fair SOL price | Wallet trade result | Pool 1 reserves, SOL value / USDC | Pool 2 reserves, SOL value / USDC |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Start | Borrow `800 USDC` | `$400 / $400` | `$400 / $400` |
| `800 USDC→SOL` in pool 1 | `$266.67` worth of SOL | `$133.33 / $1,200` | `$400 / $400` |
| SOL→USDC in pool 2 | `160 USDC` | `$133.33 / $1,200` | `$666.67 / $240` |

The wallet is `640 USDC` short of repaying the flash **if it uses route output only**, while the actor's two LPs mark up by the matching `$640` at the unchanged outside price. If it withdraws the positions, it can repay with its own returned inventory. Conversely, a public arbitrageur with `160 USDC` could buy back `$266.67` of SOL in pool 2 and sell it to pool 1 for `800 USDC`, restoring both idealized pools and making `$640` from the actor's deliberately distorted LP reserves. This limiting model deliberately excludes third-party LPs, transaction fees, and finite CLMM tick/rounding details.

An external return venue changes the second reserve and its depth. To repay atomically from **swap output alone**, the outside venue must return at least the flash principal plus any costs. If the actor **withdraws its own LP positions before repayment**, the withdrawal returns its deposited inventory and converted principal, which may enable repayment; the actor has merely recycled its own collateral. Summing wallet plus both owned LPs by mint, a wholly self-owned pair conserves SOL and USDC through fee-zero self-swaps, apart from integer rounding, transaction fees, rent, and external trades. A wallet-only route receipt is therefore not a profit statement; the LP account must be marked and ultimately withdrawn or independently valued.

### Committed two-position and zero-fee flash control

A separate fresh local mainnet-backed fork **committed this mechanism in one signed v1 transaction**, after two actor-owned full-range LPs were created and funded on the fork. The actor added `L=1,240,000,000` in each pool, contributing **3.817631995 SOL + 402.762760 USDC** to 3PRT and **3.825038662 SOL + 401.982865 USDC** to BSdd. Its token wallets were then zero before the transaction. The v1 transaction borrowed a total **6,271.347260340 WSOL** from zero-fee KLend and Project0 flash facilities, swapped WSOL→USDC in 3PRT and USDC→WSOL in BSdd, decreased both of its own positions to `L=0`, collected their fees, and fully repaid both lenders. It committed at fork slot `458883047` with `262,991` CU, `1,807` wire bytes, `37` accounts, and `10,000` lamports transaction fee. The second hop was a **direct Whirlpool instruction**; this receipt does **not** prove a Jupiter Router build would see the fork-only LP deposits or choose these pools. BSdd's trading and protocol fees were [set to zero in this same isolated fork](dual-owned-fee-zero.json) before LP funding. [Signed dual-position receipt](dual-owned-flash-control.json).

The actor's LP principal before the transaction was **7.642670657 SOL + 804.745625 USDC**, about `$1,603.40` at this comparison rate; after withdrawal and flash repayment its wallet held **7.237245518 SOL + 622.510223 USDC**, about `$1,378.80`. The exact token changes balance across the actor and the two pool vaults. The residual vault rows include existing LP inventory, old accrued claims, and rounding, so they should not be labeled as collectible profit for a particular preexisting LP without position-level settlement. In this tested direction and size, the actor **transferred value into the existing pools**, despite controlling both new LP positions and setting BSdd's future trading/protocol fees to zero on the fork. Transaction fee and any rent cost are additional to the token-value loss. No mainnet send occurred.

If outsiders hold share `e` of one pool, a self-funded price push can transfer `e` times the trade's mark-to-market loss to those outsiders. After withdrawing its own LP, the actor might reverse-swap the outsiders' inventory to fair price and recapture that same transfer in an ideal zero-fee constant-product model. Those two legs cancel before costs; leaving the position open lets public arbitrageurs take the restoration spread. Any net value from third-party inventory requires an independent market-price discrepancy or another defect, and is bounded by outsider depth and price impact. It is not a route to collect old vault residual or PR #1396 rounded-away fee claims.

### Mixed pools and unequal split

The actual raw spots differ: BSdd about `104.7470` USDC/SOL and 3PRT about `105.5007`. For a hypothetical zero-fee **wallet route** buying SOL with B in BSdd then selling SOL for B in 3PRT, define `k=P₃PRT/P_BSdd≈1.0071954`; ideal full-range virtual-reserve math gives

`B_out = k B / [1 + B(1/r_BSdd + k/r_3PRT)]`.

With `$800 TVL` of new balanced liquidity in **each** pool, initial virtual quote reserves are about `$732.184` BSdd (`$332.184` old + `$400` new) and `$400.098` 3PRT (`$0.098` old + `$400` new). The mathematical wallet-only optimum is a **~$0.925 USDC input yielding ~`$0.00332` gross USDC spread**, before fees/rounding/transaction cost. Large flash size makes this route worse. If allocating the `$1,600 TVL` unequally merely to maximize that wallet output, the old reserve imbalance suggests roughly **$466 TVL in BSdd and $1,134 in 3PRT**, giving total quote reserves `$565.1/$567.2`; the optimal wallet-only input then rises to ~$1.013 USDC and spread to only ~$0.00364. This calculation uses finalized raw spots and virtual reserves as a constant-product approximation, **not an executed route or a prediction of consolidated actor profit**. Because newly supplied BSdd liquidity would be only roughly half of its total while newly supplied 3PRT liquidity would dominate that dust pool, an apparently positive wallet route can mostly be paid for by the actor's own 3PRT LP loss. For example, marking at **104.5 USDC/SOL**, the wallet gains ~$0.00332 at that optimum, but the actor's combined wallet plus its pro-rata LP marks **lose about $0.00152**; existing LPs gain the offset. This illustrative sign changes with the outside mark and must be checked against an actual same-size outside route. The reverse direction starts from an unfavorable price ratio and cannot yield a zero-fee wallet profit at dust size. More extreme split reduces one leg's depth and its maximum output.

An outside venue near ~104.5 USDC/SOL can also route against either pool, but the opposite leg must be quoted for the **actual input size and direction**. The C7 [exact Whirlpool-to-Raydium atomic receipt](jupiter-atomic-full-boundary.json) demonstrates a Router return leg and zero-fee dual-flash envelope fitting one v1 transaction; it loses `257.483336497 SOL` and says nothing about profitability of newly owned BSdd/3PRT LPs. Likewise, a consumer using a manipulated pool's **raw spot as an oracle** could be affected if it trusts that pool and allows useful size, but no such consumer or settlement path has been identified. A Jupiter route must satisfy its executable quote/slippage; low TVL or high raw spot alone does not prove it will route a victim through the pool.

## Practical conclusion

The smallest active pool can have its displayed price moved dramatically for cents, and the fee-authorized signer can make BSdd trading and protocol fees zero on a fork. The known small pools remain full-range liquid over the tested moves and offer limited executable inventory. An actor adding its own liquidity and self-trading cannot count a wallet swap receipt as harvest without subtracting the corresponding LP principal change. The signed two-pool test repaid a large zero-fee flash but **lost about $224.60 of owned token value** while enriching the residual pool vaults. The strongest currently executed finding remains **cheap spot-state control with shallow depth**; a profitable outsider/oracle extraction needs a named consumer, an exact accepted transaction, and consolidated balance proof.
