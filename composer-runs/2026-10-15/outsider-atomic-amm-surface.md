## Direct AMM-price consumer crosswalk

The separate [Scope current-binary audit](scope-amm-consumer-current-audit.md) enumerated five Scope mapping accounts with **26 configured AMM entries across 25 distinct pools**: 16 Orca and 10 Raydium CLMM entries, zero DLMM. Of 619 current KLend reserves, 535 referenced a Scope feed, but only **one** reserve had those AMM entries directly in its configured price chain. That CTALS reserve's refreshed price was stale/invalid in the current positive control. Of 165 current marginfi Banks, one referenced a Scope feed, and that feed had no AMM entries. These are direct configuration joins; derived entries and other lenders remain unresolved.

In 25 current-binary v1 simulations, a Scope refresh after any tested prior non-Compute-Budget instruction rejected with `RefreshWithUnexpectedIxs` (6014), including prior KLend flash. `Scope refresh → flash → repay` succeeded with zero user/supply/fee deltas but could not incorporate a same-transaction AMM move before refresh. Thus this particular swap/flash → Scope → lender pricing path did not yield a complete outsider attack. A prepositioned price in an earlier transaction and independent direct-spot consumers are separate paths.

A separate [Akashi/Hastra triage](akashi-hastra-orca-consumer-triage.md) found a real direct consumer: Akashi `RefreshLeg` passes the Orca PYUSD/USDC pool into Hastra adapter `AaiValue`. A one-signer outsider-funded current-binary Orca SwapV2 → `RefreshLeg` → reverse swap succeeds in **2,292 v1 bytes, 62 keys, 264,158 CU**, changing the pool during valuation but making no Akashi payout; the wallet ends about $0.02 lower at a near-dollar mark. The exact refresh instruction itself requires 58 distinct keys. Adding the minimal KLend USDC flash state raises the union to at least 68 before any payout, beyond the 64-account v1 cap used here. Even without flash, an observed `Deposit` raises the raw union to 68 keys (at least 66 after account reuse), `RequestWithdrawal` to 67, and `FundWithdrawal` to 69. Across 915 returned successful Akashi transactions, no `ClaimWithdrawal` payout control was observed. This is a real valuation dependency, but it has no validated flash-to-payout route or outsider gain. Machine evidence: `akashi-orca-v1-no-flash.json`.

Its attributable custody is small at the latest sampled slot: the selected Akashi Hastra-leg account holds 336.876756 six-decimal GNE-share units and its idle vault holds 115.079831 USDC. The much larger accounts in the refresh are external Hastra/Orca vaults, not Akashi custody. This lead is lower priority than the ranked multi-million-dollar controllers.

The [Meteora DAMM v1 Alpha Vault consumer](damm-v1-outsider-consumer-audit.md) was checked in eight current-binary v1 paths. A complete flash → DAMM move → Alpha fill → reverse → repay envelope fits **1,441 bytes and 34 accounts**, but all 135 Alpha Vaults are past activation. Only three retain real capacity; the sole WSOL candidate's DAMM pool is disabled. Direct fill rejected `NotPermitThisActionInThisTimePoint` (6004), and that pool rejected `PoolDisabled`. The successful baseline flash/DAMM round trip fully repaid KLend and lost 395,991 raw WSOL before a modeled transaction fee. No active fill-and-profit route was established.

JanusFI does not yet enter the counted outsider transaction classes. Its direct current-binary buy/sell calls require off-curve DFlow event-authority PDA `8xea…` as signer and fail `MissingRequiredSignature` before reserve math. A diagnostic `sigVerify:false` probe promoting only that PDA to signer reaches pricing, but cannot be originated as a direct outsider transaction. Those diagnostic buy/sell cycles lose CASH at tested dust and larger amounts; four 1m-raw cycles lose 304,912 raw CASH and eight hit the instruction-trace limit. All 299 sampled routes use DFlow CPI, and the tested historical wrapper cannot be reused in current state. [Janus crosscheck](janusfi-current-math-crosscheck.md).

## Counting rule

For every future candidate, retain one row per distinct deployed producer, consumer, flash source, pool/strategy, amount boundary, and complete atomic wire. Mark it verified only when the current binary or faithful local fork shows the full flash repayment and final user/protocol balances. Do not count the same venue under separate brand names as different programs, treat sampled pools as all pools, count task dispatches as tests, or equate spot-marked custody with liquid gain.
