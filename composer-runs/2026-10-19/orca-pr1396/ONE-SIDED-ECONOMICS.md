# One-sided Whirlpool boundary: inventory and value at risk

## Mechanism

A Whirlpool range `[lower, upper)` is **all token A below its lower tick**, holds both tokens while the price is inside, and is **all token B at or above its upper tick**. The position's withdrawal amounts are computed from its liquidity, current sqrt price, and tick bounds; depositing rounds token amounts up and withdrawing rounds down. The code does not award a trader or a new LP an extra withdrawal based on a vault's remaining balance. [Liquidity token-delta calculation](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/liquidity_manager.rs#L192-L223).

An **A-to-B** swap drives price down, pays B to the trader, and converts in-range LP principal toward A. Crossing the last lower tick makes active pool liquidity zero and leaves that position all A. A **B-to-A** swap does the converse at the upper tick. The swap manager updates active liquidity when it crosses an initialized tick; its fee-growth calculation allocates swap fees only when active liquidity is positive. [Swap loop and tick crossing](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/swap_manager.rs#L89-L187), [fee-growth condition](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/swap_manager.rs#L271-L291).

**Zero active liquidity is not an empty token vault.** A vault holds principal for all positions plus recorded fee/protocol claims and rounding balances. With no active liquidity in a direction, additional swaps can traverse a zero-liquidity tick gap to the next active range, or fail/partially fill at price/tick-array limits; an opposite swap can return through the crossed tick. Existing LPs can still withdraw their one-sided principal, and an owner can add liquidity in a range covering the current price. Thus moving price outside one range does **not permanently brick** the pool. A practical outage for a route aggregator is possible while no executable liquidity exists along the requested direction; it is a state/liquidity condition, not an irreversible program failure. [Swap stopping/partial-fill behavior](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/swap_manager.rs#L90-L245), [liquidity modification](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/liquidity_manager.rs#L90-L189).

## Measured existing high-liquidity pool

I read **an already modified local mainnet-backed Surfpool fork**, `127.0.0.1:48899`, after its earlier 20,000-raw-USDC B-to-A test swap. These numbers are **not a fresh finalized mainnet snapshot** and the boundary amounts below are exact integer *token-delta arithmetic*, not a committed boundary-crossing transaction. The pool is `478r35b8cKMuj3HzxkwqWsx8vBfFLP5YhbkUEsWt2jfo`; its sole active position `6K8ebAAheXRCfzSZo8zsdoaGJe2KzjTK8UPnci6Lrgm` spans ticks `[-39573,-39567)`. Token A mint `4RpS7bthtVvog6Q9HURWTS9kFsi2qdcpBiSch9wuDsvo` and token B USDC both have **six decimals**. The original mainnet-backed fork test identifies the same pool and position and records the earlier swap: [receipt](real-mainnet-high-liquidity-pool.json).

| State in the modified local fork | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Active / sole position liquidity | `18,439,611,894,805,844,217` |
| Current tick and sqrt price X64 | `-39573`; `2,550,620,869,701,718,092` |
| Lower / upper sqrt price X64 | `2,550,620,869,701,647,909` / `2,551,386,132,483,735,115` |
| USDC vault B | `178,970` raw = `0.178970 USDC` |
| Position recorded USDC fees owed | `94,638` raw = `0.094638 USDC` |
| Protocol USDC fees owed | `14,138` raw = `0.014138 USDC` |

At the current sqrt price, the price lies only **70,183 X64 sqrt-price units** above the sole position's lower boundary. Using Whirlpool's integer amount formulas, an A-to-B move exactly to that boundary needs `3,669,540` raw A as price-moving input, plus a `367`-raw-A fee at this pool's 100/1,000,000 fee rate: **3.669907 token A total**. It pays out about **70,155 raw USDC = 0.070155 USDC**. If that swap crosses the sole lower tick and no other range becomes active, active liquidity goes to zero and the position is all A. The B vault would still hold about **108,815 raw USDC**, enough for its already recorded `94,638`-raw position fee claim plus `14,138`-raw protocol claim, leaving 39 raw USDC. This is the concrete reason one-sided position state does not mean an emptied quote vault. Exact tick prices came from Orca's `PriceMath.tickIndexToSqrtPriceX64`; token deltas use [`get_amount_delta_a/b`](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/math/token_math.rs#L60-L160).

Moving in the opposite direction from that same fork state to the position's **upper** tick is vastly larger: about `764,966,903,728,265` raw USDC as price-moving input (**764.967 million USDC**) plus a `76,504,340,807`-raw-USDC fee by the same amount formula, and would pay about `39,999,999,995,330,524` raw A. This is a mathematical boundary quote only. On-chain `u64` limits, tick-array traversal, dynamic state, available vault balances, and route size must be checked with a signed local swap before calling it executable.

## Economic interpretation

This near-lower-bound pool can be pushed out of its sole active range with a tiny A-to-B trade, but that trade extracts only about seven cents of USDC at this fork state; it is already almost entirely A. Its remaining USDC largely backs recorded fee claims and is not a freely withdrawable bounty. A large flash principal does not increase the amount payable before this tick; a swap with excess exact input either traverses further ticks/ranges or reaches a limit/partial-fill condition. To profit by selling the received USDC back for A on a separate venue, the full round trip must beat the Whirlpool fee, external venue fee, price impact, network fees, and any flash costs. No such positive cross-venue round trip has been demonstrated here.

The old PR #1396 fee-checkpoint issue is different: it can erase a fraction of an LP's fee claim under repeated permissionless updates. Crossing a range converts LP **principal** between A and B as normal CLMM behavior; it does not redirect the erased fee claim. A bug report should distinguish a temporary zero-liquidity state or price manipulation from a protocol brick or third-party harvest unless a paired control shows extra attacker receipts and lasting harm. [Existing-pool fee differential](REAL-POOL-DIFFERENTIAL.md), [harvest-path analysis](harvest-path-analysis.md).

## C7 WSOL/USDC boundary and external return

A separate **committed local Surfpool fork** test used the actual mainnet-derived C7AD8EHcbKvFL3zw2z4YKKn1ZMTCYqkThZGtY3hPajsd pool state, with A = WSOL (nine decimals) and B = USDC (six decimals). Two signed A-to-B swaps crossed a lower tick into a **zero-active-liquidity gap**. The actor spent exactly **281,243,356 WSOL lamports = 0.281243356 SOL** and received **15,568,208 raw USDC = 15.568208 USDC**. Active liquidity fell from `607,354,963` to zero at tick `-29921`, but this was **not the last quoteable range**. Pool vault A increased by those same 281,243,356 lamports, and vault B decreased by those same 15,568,208 raw USDC. Vault B still held **9,359.816004 USDC**, so this did not empty the quote vault. These transfers and state changes are executed fork receipts, **not a mainnet transaction or an atomic external return**. [C7 boundary receipt](one-sided-boundary.json).

A signed follow-up swap with just **one additional WSOL lamport** and **zero USDC output** traversed the empty gap to tick `-29953`, where an existing deeper range activated with liquidity `13,979,561,927`. A signed 59,143-raw-USDC reverse swap then paid the actor 1,174,842 WSOL lamports and restored active liquidity to `658,197,677` near tick `-29912`. This proves the zero-active-liquidity state is transitory and that deeper LP inventory remains accessible. It also shows how a trader can buy SOL cheaply at the distorted marginal price; it is not a payout to a new LP or a program brick. [C7 gap/reversal receipt](one-sided-boundary-followup.json).

The lower bands make the remaining vault balance economically significant to their **existing LPs**. A separate fresh local mainnet-backed fork test then **committed all nine A-to-B Whirlpool swaps in one signed v1 transaction**, using 17 existing tick arrays, 1,459 wire bytes, and 330,177 CU. The actor was prefunded on the fork to isolate the pool mechanics; this transaction did **not** include a flash loan or Jupiter return. It spent exactly **346,808,000,069 WSOL lamports = 346.808000069 SOL** and received **9,375,321,549 raw USDC = 9,375.321549 USDC**. Active liquidity ended at zero, tick `-39121`; the USDC vault fell from 9,375.384212 to **0.062663 USDC**. Vault A rose by the exact actor input. This is a true crossing of all observed A-to-B active bands on that fork, yet the residual B vault still is not freely claimable by a new LP. [Committed all-bands receipt](full-one-sided-boundary.json).

The swap's average proceeds were **27.03 USDC/SOL**, much worse than the initial gap-only 55.35 USDC/SOL and an outside rate near 104.5 USDC/SOL. A separate **committed, signed v1 transaction on a fresh mainnet-backed local fork** included two real zero-fee flashes, all nine C7 Whirlpool swaps, a Jupiter Router V2 return through the independent **Raydium** pool, and both flash repayments. It converted the exact **9,375.321549 USDC** Whirlpool output into **89.324663572 WSOL** and left the actor **257.483336497 WSOL poorer** in its WSOL account, in addition to native transaction fee and margin-account rent. Lender vault balances returned to their starting amounts. This measured route is a loss, not a profitable harvest. [Atomic execution and accounting](JUPITER-EXIT-ROUTE.md), [committed receipt](jupiter-atomic-full-boundary.json).

After the full lower-boundary swap, the B vault retained **62,663 raw USDC**. Checkpointing the five preexisting positions recorded **51,440 raw USDC** of LP fee claims, while the pool had **1,021 raw USDC** of protocol fees owed. The difference, **10,202 raw USDC**, is an unallocated residual in this accounting; it is not proven claimable by a new LP or by the fee authority. The opposite base vault retains principal/claims from its own side. Claim-level ownership and an explicit withdrawal transaction are required before assigning any residual to an actor. [Five-position fee-claim checkpoint](full-boundary-claims.json).

The **opposite upper boundary** is much cheaper in this pool. On a separate fresh local fork, a signed B-to-A swap of **two raw USDC units** received **eight WSOL lamports** and crossed the highest observed position upper tick at `-27912`; active liquidity became zero. The WSOL vault retained **612,279 lamports** after paying those eight, so base-vault inventory was not emptied. A subsequent signed swap specified one raw USDC input with an explicit limit at tick `-27800`; while active liquidity stayed zero, the recorded spot advanced there with **zero actual token transfer on both sides**. This is concrete spot-state manipulation through an empty range, not executable depth or a transfer of LP inventory. Which part of the 612,279-lamport residual backs recorded fees, protocol fees, or rounding requires a claim-level account reconciliation. [Upper-boundary and empty-gap receipt](upper-one-sided-boundary.json).

The independent Jupiter Router V2 build quote uses an external **Kipseli** USDC-to-WSOL venue and quotes `148,859,211` WSOL lamports for `15,568,209` raw USDC. That input exceeds the actual boundary receipt by **one raw USDC unit**, so this exact built instruction cannot spend only the observed boundary output; an exact-amount requote or prefunded raw unit is needed. Even granting the quoted output, the round trip returns only **0.148859211 SOL** after spending **0.281243356 SOL**: an actor shortfall of **0.132384145 SOL**, before transaction fees, rent, or slippage. A zero-fee flash loan can front the first leg but cannot repay this shortfall without the actor's own inventory. The observed Whirlpool effective rate is about **55.35 USDC/SOL**, versus about **104.58 USDC/SOL** implied by the independent return quote. [Jupiter build quote](jupiter-router-v2-boundary-exit.json).

This is a price move and ordinary swap exchange with the pool, not evidence that a third-party LP's funds were collected through the fee-checkpoint bug. The actor received only the exact USDC reduction from the pool vault and paid the exact WSOL increase. Marking both sides at the quoted independent rate, the aggregate pool received more WSOL value than the USDC it paid; individual LP realized P&L cannot be reconstructed from vault deltas alone, and the quote is not a proof of executable arbitrage on the mutated fork.

In plain terms, the actor sold SOL into C7 for an average **55.35 USDC/SOL** while the independent venue valued SOL near **104.58 USDC/SOL**; C7 ended at a marginal spot near **50.19 USDC/SOL** (from its recorded sqrt price and the 9/6 token decimals). At that comparison price, the actor gives the pool roughly **13.85 USDC** of value on this leg. If another trader then buys cheap SOL from C7 to restore its price, that trader can take much of the temporarily enriched pool inventory back through ordinary reverse swaps. The first mover's bad sale can therefore fund the arbitrageur's gain, with active LPs retaining their swap fee share. The exact division depends on the restoring trades and LP positions; no restoration was executed in this receipt.

## Tiny LP or ladder after the boundary

At the C7 boundary the recorded Whirlpool spot is already far below the independent return rate. An attacker could seek to supply new in-range liquidity at an unattractive price, or place several ranges within a gap, but the C7 gap has an **existing deeper range just 32 ticks below**. A marginal spot price by itself is **not a payout**. The swap loop computes each step's output from the active liquidity and the traversed price interval, and charges fees only on the amount actually consumed; it updates active liquidity at initialized ticks. Thus a tiny new position gives only tiny executable output before its range ends. A ladder increases executable depth only by contributing more principal, while exposing that principal to opposing traders. The remaining shared vault balance is not automatically available to the new LP. [Swap computation](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/math/swap_math.rs#L16-L115), [swap loop](https://github.com/orca-so/whirlpools/blob/f2a3d13fa04eb15cf5b5a309ef9b226fd5d34e36/programs/whirlpool/src/manager/swap_manager.rs#L89-L187).

There are two distinct potential effects. A consumer that blindly values assets from the pool's raw spot could read a manipulated price; whether any such consumer exists or accepts C7 as an oracle is **unproved**. A router instead must simulate executable swap output over the provided tick arrays and enforce its route/amount constraints. The cited Jupiter response selected Kipseli after Whirlpool/Orca V2 were **explicitly excluded**, and it reads mainnet rather than this modified fork. It therefore demonstrates an available external return quote, **not** that Jupiter would select or reject an attacker-made C7 ladder. Profit from victim flow would require a real taker to accept a worse-than-available route at enough size to cover the attacker’s initial boundary loss, liquidity capital, fees, and price risk; none has been shown.

Direction matters for the proposed ladder. Near 50 USDC/SOL, C7 offers **poor USDC output** to someone selling WSOL, so a best-output router with access to a roughly 104.58-USDC/SOL outside venue has no reason to select that C7 leg for the same size. C7 offers **cheap WSOL** to someone selling USDC, which could attract an arbitrageur, but the LP on that leg must sell its deposited WSOL below the outside rate. A forced route or an external application trusting raw spot is a separate hypothesis; neither is demonstrated by the Jupiter build quote.

If the attacker were the **only active LP** when arbitrageurs reverse the lower-side price move, it could earn the LP share of the pool's **0.25% swap fee** on amounts actually executed. But C7's actual one-lamport lower-gap crossing reaches an existing LP; exclusivity is **not established in that lower gap**. A correcting USDC-to-WSOL trader buys SOL at C7's roughly 50-USDC marginal price. Any attacker LP must supply its own SOL at that price; selling it far below the independent ~104.58-USDC rate overwhelms a 0.25% fee. Supplying only a dust position bounds this adverse principal conversion, but also bounds the fee and the executable quote. This is possible fee capture on the attacker's own capital, not a harvest of existing LP principal or rounded-away fee claims.

An executed local-fork control isolates the **sole attacker LP** case after the cheap upper-boundary price move. At tick `-27800` with zero active liquidity, an attacker opened a narrow position at `[-27480,-27464]` and deposited **3,159,260 WSOL lamports** (zero USDC) for liquidity `1,000,000,000`. An independent USDC-to-WSOL trader crossed the empty gap with zero token transfer and then bought that position's full WSOL inventory, paying **203,067 raw USDC** for **3,159,259 lamports**. The attacker position's checkpoint recorded **502 raw USDC of LP fees**; the pool's protocol claim rose by **5 raw USDC**. Its remaining principal is approximately 202,559 raw USDC; a new LP did **not** receive the old vault's 612,279-lamport WSOL residual (which became 612,280 through rounding). At the independent ~104.58-USDC/SOL rate, the deposited WSOL was worth about **0.330 USDC**, against about **0.203 USDC** gained including its fee: roughly **0.127 USDC mark-to-market loss**, before rent. This is a real quote controlled by the attacker, but it is adverse principal conversion, not profitable fee harvesting. The LP has not withdrawn or settled that principal in this receipt. [Sole-LP control](tiny-lp-after-boundary.json).
